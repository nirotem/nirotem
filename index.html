About me

I am a sociologist examining the construction and transformation of social knowledge and the contestations involved in its global circulation. My work lies at the intersection of transnational sociology, the sociology of knowledge, and political sociology.

Currently, I am a David and Rosa Orzen Research Fellow at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. I earned my PhD from the University of Minnesota (2022). My doctoral dissertation investigated gradual shifts in the humanitarian idea through a historical ethnography of the UNHCR, tracking the infiltration of development and human rights knowledge into the field.

My research agenda is driven by an interest in the role of institutions and the dynamics between culture and knowledge. I utilize a diverse methodological toolkit, encompassing historical ethnography, archival work, and quantitative approaches including survey analysis, regression modeling, and machine learning.
