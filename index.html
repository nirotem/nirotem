About me

I am a sociologist of education and knowledge specializing in higher education systems, the sociology of science, and transnational knowledge dynamics. Informed by neoinstitutional theory and the sociology of knowledge, my research examines educational and scientific institutions as primary societal arenas where policy models, epistemic authority, and cultural boundaries are constructed, negotiated, and contested.

Currently, I am a David and Rosa Orzen Research Fellow at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. I completed my Ph.D. in Sociology at the University of Minnesota in 2022. My scholarly commitment to education research began during my formative years at the Hebrew University's School of Education, where I conducted applied research at the NCJW Research Institute for Innovation in Education.

My research agenda is structured around three intersecting pillars:

  • Higher Education Policy, Retention & Equity: Unpacking institutional predictors of student persistence—including "existential dropouts" and master's degree attrition—and evaluating access pathways to show that affirmative action expansion does not compromise graduation outcomes, alongside advocating for sustainable "fund-and-flourish" academic labor models.
  • Sociology of Science & Epistemic Transformations: Analyzing how technological transformations, emerging computational cultures, and generative AI reconfigure scientific practice, disciplinary cultures, and academic labor.
  • Global Knowledge Dynamics & Illiberalism: Investigating the circulation of global policy scripts, the erosion of academic freedom and institutional autonomy under shifting political regimes, and the relationship between democratic governance and public trust in science.

Across these areas, I employ a diverse methodological repertoire that integrates quantitative analytics (predictive modeling, longitudinal panel data, and survey research) with qualitative depth (historical ethnography and in-depth interviewing).
